# Study Plan

## Status

**Study setup complete. Participant sessions pending.**

This document is a plan, not a report of completed research.

## Research objective

Evaluate whether first-time or infrequent users can complete the Cedarline claim-reporting flow while:

- understanding what is required now versus later;
- handling uncertain information without feeling blocked;
- deferring photos/documents;
- reviewing and correcting information before submission;
- understanding what submission does and does not mean.

## Research questions

1. Do users understand where to begin?
2. Do policy and loss-type choices provide enough information scent?
3. Can users comfortably choose unknown / unsure states?
4. Do users understand that photos can be added later?
5. Can users find and use review/edit controls?
6. Does the confirmation state clearly explain what happens next?
7. Where do participants hesitate, backtrack, ask for help, or misinterpret terminology?

## Method

Each 35–45 minute moderated session combines:

1. consent;
2. 5-minute pre-task interview;
3. task-based usability testing;
4. 1–7 ease rating after each task;
5. short post-study questionnaire;
6. 5-minute debrief interview.

## Planned sample

Five adults recruited through a convenience sample.

The study does not claim statistical representativeness. The goal is rapid formative feedback and evidence of research practice.

Participants do not need insurance-industry expertise.

Record for context only:
- device used;
- whether they have ever filed an insurance claim;
- self-rated comfort using web forms.

Do not collect policy numbers, claim numbers, medical details, financial information, or other unnecessary sensitive data.

## Prototype

Cedarline Claim Reporting UX:
https://github.com/joshuaokent-spec/cedarline-claim-reporting-ux

Use the same stable prototype version for all participants unless a critical defect prevents testing. If the prototype changes mid-study, document which participants saw each version.

## Success signals

This formative study is not pass/fail for the design. Examine:

- independent task completion;
- moderator assistance;
- critical errors;
- backtracking;
- hesitation;
- post-task ease ratings;
- comments indicating misunderstanding or confidence.

## Ethics and privacy

- Participation is voluntary.
- Participants can stop at any time.
- Use participant IDs P01–P05 in public artifacts.
- Do not commit raw recordings or personally identifying notes to GitHub.
- Obtain separate explicit permission before audio/screen recording.
- Public case-study evidence should be anonymized and paraphrased unless quote permission is explicitly obtained.

## Deliverables after research

- anonymized session summaries;
- synthesis notes;
- severity-ranked findings;
- redesign priorities;
- before/after screens;
- limitations;
- final case-study update.
