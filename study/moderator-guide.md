# Moderator Guide

## Before the session

- Open the same stable Claim Reporting prototype version used for the study.
- Open the local study runner.
- Assign the next participant ID (P01–P05).
- Close unrelated tabs and notifications.
- If recording, verify recording consent before starting.

## Opening

Read the consent script.

Then say:

> I’m testing the design, not you. As you work, please say what you’re looking at, what you expect, and anything that feels unclear. I may stay quiet while you work because I want to see what the interface communicates on its own.

## Pre-task interview

Ask without turning the answers into a tutorial:

1. Have you ever had to report damage or file an insurance claim? No private details are needed.
2. If you had to report a car accident online, what information would you expect the insurer to ask for first?
3. What would make an online claim form feel stressful or difficult?
4. How comfortable are you with long web forms: 1 (not comfortable) to 5 (very comfortable)?

## During tasks

Use neutral prompts.

Preferred:
- “What are you thinking?”
- “What would you do next?”
- “What did you expect to happen?”
- “What are you looking for?”

Avoid:
- “Did you see the button?”
- “You should click Review.”
- “The right answer is Unsure.”
- teaching terminology the interface is supposed to communicate.

## Assistance rule

Allow the participant to struggle long enough to reveal the issue.

If help is necessary:
1. give a general prompt;
2. then a directional hint;
3. only then explain the specific control.

Record each assistance event.

## After every task

Ask:

> On a scale from 1 to 7, where 1 is very difficult and 7 is very easy, how easy or difficult was that task?

Record the rating before discussing the task in depth.

## Debrief interview

Ask:

1. What part felt easiest?
2. What part felt most confusing or uncertain?
3. Was there anywhere you expected different wording or information?
4. Did anything make you feel like you needed information you might not have during a real accident?
5. How confident would you feel submitting this report in a real situation?
6. If you could change one thing, what would it be?

## After the participant leaves

- Finish notes while memory is fresh.
- Mark critical incidents.
- Separate observation from interpretation.
- Export/save the session locally.
- Do not commit raw participant data to the public repository.
